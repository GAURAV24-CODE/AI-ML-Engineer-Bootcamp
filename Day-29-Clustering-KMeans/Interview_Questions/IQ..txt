DAY 29/45 - K-MEANS CLUSTERING INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
===================================================

BASIC QUESTIONS
---------------

Q1. What is K-Means clustering?
Ans: K-Means is an unsupervised machine learning algorithm that
groups similar data points into K clusters.

Q2. Why is K-Means called unsupervised learning?
Ans: Because it works with unlabeled data and discovers patterns
without a target column.

Q3. What is K in K-Means?
Ans: K represents the number of clusters to create.

Q4. What is a centroid?
Ans: The mean position of all data points assigned to a cluster.

Q5. What are the main steps of K-Means?
Ans: Choose K, initialize centroids, assign points, update
centroids, and repeat until convergence.

INTERMEDIATE QUESTIONS
----------------------

Q6. How do you select the best value of K?
Ans: Use the Elbow Method, Silhouette Score, and domain knowledge.

Q7. What is the Elbow Method?
Ans: It plots inertia against K and looks for the point where
the reduction in inertia begins to slow down.

Q8. What is inertia?
Ans: The sum of squared distances between data points and their
assigned cluster centroids.

Q9. Why is feature scaling important in K-Means?
Ans: K-Means uses distance calculations. Features with larger
numerical ranges can dominate without scaling.

Q10. What is K-Means++?
Ans: An initialization method that selects starting centroids
more strategically.

Q11. What is the Silhouette Score?
Ans: A metric that measures how well points fit their own cluster
compared with neighboring clusters. It ranges from -1 to +1.

Q12. Is K-Means sensitive to outliers?
Ans: Yes. Outliers can pull centroids away from the main groups.

ADVANCED QUESTIONS
------------------

Q13. What are the limitations of K-Means?
Ans: It requires K in advance, is sensitive to outliers and
initialization, and may struggle with irregular-shaped clusters.

Q14. Can K-Means handle categorical data directly?
Ans: Standard K-Means is designed for numerical features.
Categorical data needs suitable encoding, and other algorithms
may be more appropriate.

Q15. What is the difference between K-Means and Hierarchical
Clustering?
Ans: K-Means forms K clusters using centroids. Hierarchical
Clustering builds a hierarchy of clusters, often visualized
using a dendrogram.

Q16. What does random_state=42 do?
Ans: It makes randomized initialization reproducible.

Q17. What is the difference between fit() and predict()?
Ans: fit() learns the clusters. predict() assigns new points to
the learned clusters.

Q18. What does fit_predict() do?
Ans: It fits the model and returns cluster labels for the data.

PRACTICAL QUESTIONS
-------------------

Q19. Give one real-world application of K-Means.
Ans: Customer segmentation based on income and spending score.

Q20. What happens when K is too large?
Ans: The data may be split into too many small clusters, making
the groups less useful.

Q21. Does lower inertia always mean a better model?
Ans: No. Inertia usually decreases as K increases, so use other
metrics and domain knowledge to select K.

Q22. How do you assign a new customer to a cluster?
Ans: Scale the new customer's features using the fitted scaler
and pass them to the trained model's predict() method.
